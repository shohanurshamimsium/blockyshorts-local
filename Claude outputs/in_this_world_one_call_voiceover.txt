In this world, you get one phone call to someone who has died. Three minutes. They always pick up. Most people waste it.

My cousin Ray called his dead grandfather to ask where he hid the spare key. Grandpa said, under the mat. There was never a key under the mat.

My teacher called her dead husband to finish an argument from nineteen eighty-nine. He never got a word in.

My dad never used his. Mom died when I was seven, and everyone told him, call her. He always said the same thing. Not yet.

He answered every call on the first ring, like he was waiting for a different one.

When I turned eighteen, I begged him. Dad, just call her. He laughed and fixed my collar. Save it for something that matters.
